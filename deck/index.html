The solution

One flow. Two signatures. No BTC for gas.

01

Choose an amount

Draw it from your Mezo trove, or use MUSD already in your wallet. The naira amount is shown up front at Paycrest's live rate.

02

Pick your bank

Any of 174 Nigerian banks. Owo reads the account name back to you before you sign.

03

Sign twice

No transactions to send. Your trove mints MUSD straight into a locked order on OwoDesk, and Owo's relayer pays the gas.

04

Get paid

The desk pays the naira out through Paycrest, an open stablecoin-to-bank network, and only then takes your MUSD.

Safety

Your MUSD is released only after the naira is sent.

A signed intent can't be redirected

The EIP-712 CashOut intent binds:

owneramountnaira floorbank-details hash

A relayed signature can't pay someone else's account, or pay you less.

Bank details stay off-chain

Only a salted hash goes on Mezo. The details are RSA-encrypted to Paycrest's key.

Limits and a pause switch

Min and max order size, a daily cap, pause. Users can always exit, even while paused.

Two things we found the hard way

withdrawMUSD to a contract

withdrawMUSD always makes a native call to the recipient for the collateral change, which is 0 BTC when only MUSD is drawn. A contract recipient needs a receive().

OwoDesk accepts zero-value calls and refuses real BTC.

Redemption hints can overshoot

HintHelpers.getRedemptionHints keeps walking past a skipped partial redemption, so its truncated amount can be more than TroveManager will take, and the call reverts.

Owo walks SortedTroves itself and sizes the batch to what will actually redeem.

Business model

A fee per cash-out, and a cheaper exit.

1%

Owo fee per cash-out

Shown up front in the quote. It covers the desk's 0.75% redemption exit plus gas, leaving about 0.25% margin. The user still gets more naira than selling on Mezo's DEX.

Who it's for

Nigerians who hold BTC and need naira for rent and bills, without selling the BTC. Mezo's kickoff Q&A named borrow-to-bank-account as a Track 2 fit.
